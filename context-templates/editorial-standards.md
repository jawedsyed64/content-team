# Editorial Standards

> These are sensible defaults for any publisher. Review and adjust them.

## Research and fact-checking requirements
- Every statistic, figure, date, quotation and named study must be traceable to
  a source link gathered during research.
- Claims that cannot be verified are removed or clearly labelled as opinion.
- Statistics older than 3 years are flagged with their date.
- Quotations must be verbatim and attributed; never paraphrase inside quotation
  marks.
- No invented statistics, quotations, people, studies or references — ever.

## Originality standards
- Content is written fresh from the research brief, not copied from sources.
- Direct quotations from any source are short and attributed.
- Each piece must add something: a clear angle, synthesis, examples or advice.

## Source reliability criteria
- Preferred: government and intergovernmental bodies, peer-reviewed research,
  official company publications, established news organisations.
- Acceptable with care: industry reports (note who funded them), reputable
  trade publications.
- Avoid: anonymous blogs, content farms, unsourced social media posts, AI-
  generated pages, sites selling the thing they are reviewing.

## Quality assessment criteria
Scored 1–10 each: accuracy and reliability; relevance to the topic; readability
and clarity; grammar and language; brand voice consistency; SEO optimisation;
originality and usefulness. Any score below 7 needs a specific explanation and
recommendation.

## Approval and revision procedures
- The Editorial Quality Manager must not approve content with any unresolved
  factual problem.
- Content returned for revision goes back to the Writer with specific,
  numbered revision requests.
- Final approval always rests with the human editor (you).
